Running for city council

How many votes does it take to win a city council seat?

Council races are decided by a few hundred votes, which means a candidate who knows the number and knocks the doors can beat a better-known opponent who doesn't.

The number is smaller than it looks

People picture a city council election as the whole city voting. In a ward race, it's one ward, in an off year, with turnout that often runs between 15% and 25%. A ward of 4,200 registered voters might cast 900 ballots. You need about half of them.

At-large seats cover the whole city and the numbers are bigger, but the math is the same: ballots expected, times the share you need, plus a cushion. The win number guide explains each step.

What 487 votes takes

Using the standard planning assumptions (half the votes from voter contact, a 50% support rate, one door in four answering, 15 doors an hour), 487 votes means about 1,360 door knocks and 91 hours at the door. Over 12 weeks that's about three three-hour shifts a week. A candidate can do that alone, and a candidate with two volunteers can do it twice.

The budget for a ward this size is usually $1,500 to $2,500, mostly for two or three mail pieces to the 600 or so households that vote in municipal elections, plus signs and a palm card. The budget guide shows the line items.

Three things that change the math

Runoffs and majority rules. Some cities require a majority to win, with a runoff between the top two if nobody gets it. Plan for half plus one no matter how many candidates are running, and plan for a second election with its own, lower turnout.

Multi-seat at-large races. If voters pick three council members from a field of six, your win number isn't half the ballots; it's whatever it took to finish third last time. Use the last election's result instead of turnout math.

Primaries. In partisan cities, the primary is often the real election. Primary turnout runs around 20% or lower, and your win number is half plus one of your own party's primary voters, not the whole ward.

Where to get the numbers

Your city or county clerk publishes registered voters by ward and the results of past council elections. Ask for the last three municipal elections, not just the last one, so you can see whether turnout is rising or falling. Many clerks will also sell or give you the list of who voted, which is the best door-knocking list you can get.

Timeline

Most council candidates start too late. Filing is often in the spring for a fall election, but the people who win are knocking doors by early summer, finishing persuasion before early voting opens, and spending the last two weeks reminding supporters to vote. Twelve weeks of doors is a good plan; eight is the minimum. The campaign plan guide lays out the calendar.

Questions

How many votes do you need to win a city council election?

It depends on the ward's size and turnout. A ward of 4,200 registered voters in an off-year election with about 22% turnout casts around 924 ballots, so a two-candidate race needs 463 votes, or about 487 with a 5% cushion.

How much does it cost to run for city council?

A single-ward race typically costs $1,500 to $2,500, mostly for mail to likely-voter households, plus yard signs and a palm card. At-large races in larger cities cost more because mail replaces door-knocking.

How many doors should a city council candidate knock?

For a win number of 487, about 1,360 door knocks, or roughly 91 volunteer hours, which is about three three-hour shifts a week over 12 weeks.
